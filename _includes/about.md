
I am a Ph.D. student in Geosciences at [Virginia Tech](https://www.vt.edu/) working with Dr. Mike Willis, but my foundation is in engineering. I design and integrate radar systems, build field instruments, and develop the signal processing methods that turn raw signals into measurements of the Earth.

My current research focuses on ground-based GNSS passive radar for monitoring unstable rock slopes in Greenland. I develop the field instruments and processing methods that turn satellite reflections into terrain-referenced radar images, with the longer-term goal of measuring slope deformation.

Before Virginia Tech, I received my B.S. and M.S. degrees from the Department of Electronics Engineering at [Pusan National University](https://www.pusan.ac.kr/eng/Main.do) in South Korea and spent two years at the [Agency for Defense Development](https://www.add.re.kr/eps), where I worked on a SAR jamming project. That experience shaped my interest in radar signal processing, practical system development, and the connection between algorithms and real-world measurements.
